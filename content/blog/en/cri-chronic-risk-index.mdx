The **CRI (Chronic Risk Index)** assesses the long-term health risk associated with repeated exposure to pesticides. It targets populations exposed professionally or through proximity to treated areas, and integrates the intrinsic chronic toxicity properties of active substances as well as their environmental persistence.

## What the CRI measures

The CRI combines two components:

- **75%** — Active substance
- **25%** — Secondary metabolites (maximum risk among all referenced metabolites)

## Active substance (75%)

### Chronic toxicity

| Criterion evaluated |
|---|
| Carcinogenicity |
| Endocrine disruption |
| Reproductive toxicity |
| Mitochondrial damage |
| Neurotoxicity |
| Genotoxicity |
| Chronic organ damage |
| CLP hazard statements of the pesticide |

### Exposure by population

| Factor |
|---|
| Environmental persistence (soil, plant, living tissues) |
| Molecule volatility |
| PFA (forever pollutant) |

## Secondary metabolites (25%)

Same chronic toxicity and exposure criteria — the maximum value is taken among all referenced metabolites (PPDB / BPDB).

Exposure factors include: environmental persistence and potential presence of PFA.

## PFA rule (forever pollutant)

If the active substance or one of its metabolites is a PFAS, all exposure factors are automatically raised to their maximum value.

## How to read a CRI value

A high CRI signals a substance with proven chronic toxicity properties **and** high environmental persistence — which increases the duration and repetition of exposure for the populations concerned.

| CRI level | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Very high | Strong chronic toxicity + high persistence and/or volatility |
| High | Significant chronic toxicity or prolonged exposure |
| Medium | Moderate risk — caution in case of repeated treatments |
| Low / Very low | Limited chronic toxicity and rapid degradation |

## Data sources

- **PPDB** (Pesticide Properties DataBase) — toxicology and persistence module
- **CLP Regulation** — chronic hazard classifications
- **European regulatory dossiers** for active substances (EFSA)

## Key difference with the ARI

The **ARI** measures the risk of a single exposure event (application). The **CRI** measures the cumulative risk associated with repeated exposures over time — relevant for assessing long-term occupational risks and chronic exposure reduction policies.
