The **ARI (Acute Risk Index)** measures the immediate health risk associated with the use of a plant protection product. It distinguishes two exposed populations according to their mode and level of contact with the product or the treated crop.

## What the ARI measures

The ARI is calculated for two populations:

- **Applicator, workers and bystanders** — exposure during application and to volatile molecules
- **Re-entry personnel** — skin contact with the treated crop after application

## Breakdown by population

### Applicator, workers and bystanders

| Factor | Data used |
|---|---|
| Acute toxicity | Dermal toxicity, inhalation toxicity, eye irritation, allergy |

### Re-entry personnel

| Factor | Data used |
|---|---|
| Acute toxicity | Dermal toxicity |

## Exposure by population

Exposure factors are common to both populations and modulated according to the exposure profile:

- Molecule volatility
- Persistence in the crop
- Solid or liquid formulation of the product

## How to read an ARI value

A high ARI signals that a product presents significant acute toxicity **and/or** high exposure for at least one of the two populations.

| ARI level | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Very high | Strong acute toxicity + high exposure (volatility or plant persistence) |
| High | Significant toxicity or high exposure, but not both simultaneously |
| Medium | Moderate risk, vigilance during application |
| Low / Very low | Limited toxicity and rapid degradation |

## Data sources

- **PPDB** (Pesticide Properties DataBase) — toxicology module
- **CLP Regulation** — hazard classifications
- **European regulatory dossiers** for active substances (EFSA)

## Key difference with IFT and EIQ

The **IFT** (Treatment Frequency Index) measures usage pressure but ignores toxicity. The **EIQ** integrates a health dimension but without distinguishing exposed populations. The ARI **differentiates populations** (applicator/bystanders vs re-entry personnel) and weights the risk by the doses actually applied.
