The **CORI (Consumer Risk Index)** quantifies the risk of chronic exposure of consumers to pesticide residues — via food (CORIcrop) and via drinking water (CORIwater). It integrates both the toxicological properties of active substances and their ability to persist and migrate within the crop and groundwater compartments.

## What the CORI measures

The CORI is the sum of two complementary sub-indicators:

**CORI = CORIcrop + CORIwater**

Each sub-indicator combines:

- **75%** — Active substance
- **25%** — Secondary metabolites (maximum among all referenced metabolites)

## CORIcrop — Crop contamination

### Active substance (75%)

| Factor | Data used |
|---|---|
| **Chronic toxicity** | Carcinogenicity, endocrine disruption, reproductive toxicity, mitochondrial damage, neurotoxicity, genotoxicity, chronic organ damage, CLP hazard statements of the pesticide |
| **Crop exposure** | Persistence in crop, persistence in soil, systemicity of the pesticide, PFA (forever pollutant) |

### Secondary metabolites (25%)

Same toxicity and exposure criteria — maximum value taken among all metabolites (PPDB / BPDB).

## CORIwater — Groundwater contamination

### Active substance (75%)

| Factor | Data used |
|---|---|
| **Chronic toxicity** | Same criteria as CORIcrop |
| **Leaching exposure** | Mobility, persistence in soil, PFA (forever pollutant) |

### Secondary metabolites (25%)

Same toxicity and exposure criteria — maximum value taken among all metabolites (PPDB / BPDB).

## PFA rule (forever pollutant)

If the active substance or one of its metabolites is a PFAS, all exposure factors are raised to their maximum value for both sub-indicators.

## How to read a CORI value

The CORI reflects the potential residual risk to the end consumer. A high **CORIcrop** indicates that the substance persists on the plant and has chronic toxicological properties. A high **CORIwater** signals a risk of groundwater contamination, particularly for highly mobile molecules.

| CORI level | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Very high | Strong chronic toxicity + high persistence in crop and/or groundwater |
| High | Toxicity or persistence predominant in one of the two compartments |
| Medium | Moderate risk, to be monitored in water catchment areas |
| Low / Very low | Rapid degradation and/or limited chronic toxicity |

## Data sources

- **PPDB** (Pesticide Properties DataBase) — residues and soil module
- **European regulatory dossiers** (EFSA)
- **CLP classifications**

## Key difference with MRLs

**MRLs** (Maximum Residue Limits) set a regulatory threshold per substance and per crop, but do not allow comparison of the overall impact of a pesticide program. The CORI aggregates all substances applied over a schedule, incorporating chronic toxicity and contamination pathways, to produce a synthetic score that is comparable between treatment programs.
